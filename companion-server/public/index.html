01 The Trap

A network you trust.

Your phone picks WiFi by its name. That name is not unique and not verified. Anyone can broadcast it.

📶Kaffi_FRIconnecting…

Why does it just connect?

On WPA2, an access point only proves it knows the password. It never proves who it is. Clone the name, know the password, and you are indistinguishable from the real one.

clone accepted ✓

That one gap is the whole attack. WPA3 with Protected Management Frames is the fix, which is exactly why this is shown on WPA2.

06 The Verdict

This isn't hypothetical.

The same chip has really been read, crashed, and owned in the wild. Tap a case.

Remote takeover, no pairing

BrakTooth · CVE-2021-28139
Code execution on the ESP32 from radio range, with no credentials at all. 2021.

One frame crashes it

CVE-2019-12588
A crafted WiFi frame crashes the ESP stack, the same radio this twin uses.

Radio-range DoS

SweynTooth · CVE-2020-13594
Invalid packets crash or deadlock the ESP32 Bluetooth stack from across the room.

Secrets glitched out

CVE-2019-17391
Fault injection recovers the flash-encryption keys. Even "protected" flash fell on old silicon.

Hardcoded creds at scale

Mirai · 2016
Default passwords in ~150,000 devices took down half the internet at 1.2 Tbps.
